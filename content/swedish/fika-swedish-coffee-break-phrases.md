If you move to Sweden, one of the first Swedish words you will hear is **fika**. Your new colleagues will ask you to fika, your neighbour will invite you for fika, and every café will sell it. Fika is coffee (or tea) with something sweet, but above all it is a pause you take *together*. Learning the words around it is one of the fastest ways into everyday Swedish, and into Swedish friendships.

## What is fika?

**Fika** is both a noun and a verb: *en fika* (a coffee break) and *att fika* (to have one). You can fika at home, in a café, outdoors on a sunny day, or in the **fikarum** (break room) at work. Many workplaces have a fixed **fikapaus**, often in the middle of the morning and again in the afternoon.

What you eat matters less than taking the time. Classic choices are a **kanelbulle** (cinnamon bun), a **kardemummabulle** (cardamom bun), **kladdkaka** (sticky chocolate cake) or, for a celebration, **prinsesstårta** (princess cake with its green marzipan).

## Words for the fika table

| Swedish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| en kopp kaffe | a cup of coffee | Jag tar en kopp kaffe, tack. |
| te | tea | Vill du ha te eller kaffe? |
| mjölk | milk | Med mjölk, tack. |
| en kanelbulle | a cinnamon bun | En kanelbulle, tack! |
| en kaka | a cookie, a cake | Kakan är jättegod. |
| påtår | a refill (of coffee) | Ingår påtår? |
| god / gott | tasty, good | Det var gott! |

**Påtår** is a very Swedish word: a second cup of coffee, often free in cafés. *Ingår påtår?* means "Is a refill included?"

## Phrases to join in

- **Ska vi fika?** – Shall we have a fika?
- **Har du tid för en fika?** – Do you have time for a fika?
- **Kan jag få en kaffe och en kanelbulle?** – Can I have a coffee and a cinnamon bun?
- **Jag bjuder!** – My treat!
- **Tack för fikat!** – Thanks for the fika! (said when you leave)

*Tack för fikat* follows a pattern Swedes use all the time: **tack för** + the thing you shared. *Tack för maten* (thanks for the food), *tack för i dag* (thanks for today), *tack för senast* (thanks for last time).

## Grammar corner: en and ett

Every Swedish noun is either an **en** word or an **ett** word, and you simply have to learn which one. Most nouns are en words.

| Indefinite | Definite | English |
|---|---|---|
| en kopp | koppen | a cup / the cup |
| en bulle | bullen | a bun / the bun |
| ett glas | glaset | a glass / the glass |
| ett bord | bordet | a table / the table |

Notice that "the" is not a separate word in Swedish: it goes on the end of the noun. *Koppen är varm* – the cup is hot.

## Fika like a local

- If someone at work says *Det är fika!*, it is an invitation, not an announcement. Join in, even for five minutes: this is where colleagues get to know each other.
- Do not take the last bun on the plate without asking: *Får jag ta den sista?* (May I take the last one?)
- On **4 October**, Sweden celebrates **Kanelbullens dag**, Cinnamon Bun Day. Bakeries are full, and so are the fikarum tables.
