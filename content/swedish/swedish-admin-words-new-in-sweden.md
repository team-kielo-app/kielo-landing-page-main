The first month in a new country is mostly forms, queues and phone calls. In Sweden almost all of it happens in Swedish, even when the person helping you would switch to English. Knowing the key words makes every letter, website and appointment less stressful. Here are the ones you will meet first.

## The number everything depends on

Your **personnummer** (personal identity number) is the key to life in Sweden: a bank account, a phone contract, healthcare and **BankID** (the digital ID Swedes use to log in almost everywhere) all ask for it. You get it from **Skatteverket**, the Swedish Tax Agency, when you are registered in the **folkbokföring** (population register). This usually requires that you plan to live in Sweden for at least a year. People staying for a shorter time can get a **samordningsnummer** (coordination number) instead.

## Offices and services

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Skatteverket | the Swedish Tax Agency (also handles the population register) |
| Migrationsverket | the Swedish Migration Agency |
| Försäkringskassan | the Swedish Social Insurance Agency |
| Arbetsförmedlingen | the Swedish Public Employment Service |
| kommunen | the municipality (your local council) |
| vårdcentralen | the health centre |
| 1177 | the national healthcare advice line and website |

## Words on forms and letters

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| ett ID-kort | an ID card |
| en blankett | a form |
| en ansökan | an application |
| ett beslut | a decision |
| en kö | a queue |
| ett hyreskontrakt | a rental contract |
| ett andrahandskontrakt | a sublet contract |
| en adress | an address |

Housing in Sweden comes with its own vocabulary. A **förstahandskontrakt** is a lease directly with the landlord, often only available after years in a **bostadskö** (housing queue), which is why so many newcomers start with an **andrahandskontrakt**, a sublet.

## Phrases that help

- **Jag har inget personnummer än.** – I don't have a personal number yet.
- **Kan jag boka en tid?** – Can I book an appointment?
- **Var ska jag skriva under?** – Where do I sign?
- **Jag förstår inte. Kan du prata lite långsammare?** – I don't understand. Could you speak a bit more slowly?
- **Kan du upprepa det, tack?** – Could you repeat that, please?
- **Talar du engelska?** – Do you speak English?

Asking someone to slow down is completely fine, and most people will happily do so. Trying in Swedish first is usually met with a smile.

## Learning Swedish: SFI

Once you are registered, you can usually join **SFI**, **Svenska för invandrare** (Swedish for immigrants), through your municipality. It is free, and classes run at different paces. Many learners combine SFI with daily practice on their phone, where they can repeat words and conversations as often as they need.
