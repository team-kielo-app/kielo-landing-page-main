# Mistä pidät? Expressing Likes and Dislikes in Finnish for Beginners

Ever wondered what makes a Finn's heart sing or their brow furrow? Understanding what people like and dislike is a cornerstone of connection, and in Finland, it often reveals a deep appreciation for nature, simplicity, and authenticity. Whether it's the quiet solitude of a lakeside cottage, the invigorating heat of a sauna, or the perfect cup of coffee, Finnish preferences are deeply rooted in their unique culture and way of life. This guide will take you on a journey into the heart of Finnish likes and dislikes, offering not just cultural insights but also essential Finnish phrases for beginners to express your own opinions.

## Embracing *Sisu* and the Simple Joys of Life

Finnish culture is often characterized by "sisu" – a unique concept encompassing grit, resilience, and determination. But alongside this strength lies a profound appreciation for life's simple pleasures. Finns find immense joy in the natural world. Forests and thousands of sparkling lakes are not just scenery; they are places for relaxation, foraging, and year-round activities. Many Finns cherish spending time at their summer cottages (*mökki*) by a lake, far from the hustle and bustle, where they can swim, fish, and simply *be*. This connection to nature is a deep-seated cultural preference. Outdoor activities like cross-country skiing, Nordic walking, and foraging for berries and mushrooms are popular hobbies.

Coffee, too, holds a special place in the Finnish heart. Finland consistently ranks among the top coffee-consuming nations in the world. It's more than just a drink; it's a ritual, a social lubricant, and a source of comfort throughout the day. You’ll often hear the inviting aroma of *kahvi* (coffee) brewing in homes and offices across the country. And, of course, there’s the sauna. It's not merely a bath but a sacred space for physical and mental cleansing, often enjoyed with family and friends. It's a place for quiet reflection and deep relaxation, embodying a truly beloved Finnish tradition.

## From Arctic Delicacies to Quiet Conversations

Finnish culinary preferences are a delightful blend of rustic traditions and fresh, natural ingredients. Staples like rye bread (*ruisleipä*), fresh fish, wild berries, and mushrooms feature prominently. Karelian pies (*karjalanpiirakka*) with egg butter (*munavoi*) are a beloved treat. While adventurous eaters might enjoy reindeer, there's also a growing appreciation for vegetarian and diverse international cuisines. Finns generally appreciate good, honest food that celebrates the flavors of the land.

When it comes to social interaction, Finns often value quiet and authenticity. Small talk is less common and sometimes even seen as unnecessary. Instead, Finns prefer meaningful conversations and comfortable silence. This doesn't mean they're unfriendly; quite the opposite. Once you've earned a Finn's trust, you'll find them incredibly loyal and warm. They tend to dislike pretentiousness or showing off, preferring humility and genuine character. Hobbies like reading, walking, and exercising in nature are popular ways to spend leisure time. Ice hockey, while watched by many, still takes a backseat to more solitary pursuits for many Finns.

## 🇫🇮 Language Corner / Kielinurkka

Now that you have a better understanding of Finnish culture and preferences, let's learn how to express your own likes and dislikes in Finnish! The key verbs you'll need are *pitää* and *tykätä*, both meaning "to like." These verbs are always followed by the **-sta/-stä** case ending, which indicates "from" or "about" something. This might seem a little unusual at first, as it literally translates to "to hold from" or "to like from."

![A colorful illustration of Finnish language flashcards featuring phrases like "Mistä pidät?", "Pidän kahvista," and "En pidä melusta," surrounded by small doodles of Finnish cultural elements like a sauna bucket, a berry, and a lake.](/blogs/images/2026-08-20-mista-pidat-finnish-likes-dislikes-img2.webp)

### Useful Phrases

-   ***Mistä pidät?*** - What do you like?
-   ***Pidän kahvista.*** - I like coffee.
-   ***En pidä kiireestä.*** - I don't like rushing/hurry.
-   ***Tykkään luonnosta.*** - I like nature. (More colloquial than *pitää*)
-   ***Etkö pidä saunasta?*** - Don't you like the sauna?

### Vocabulary / Sanasto

| Finnish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *pitää* | to like (formal/general) | *Minä pidän urheilusta.* (I like sports.) |
| *tykätä* | to like (colloquial) | *Hän tykkää pizzasta.* (He/She likes pizza.) |
| *rakastaa* | to love | *Rakastan Suomea!* (I love Finland!) |
| *en pidä* | I don't like | *En pidä sateesta.* (I don't like rain.) |
| *herkullinen* | delicious | *Tämä ruoka on herkullista.* (This food is delicious.) |
| *mukava* | nice, pleasant | *Mukava ilta!* (A pleasant evening!) |
| *kaunis* | beautiful | *Suomen luonto on kaunis.* (Finnish nature is beautiful.) |
| *melu* | noise | *En tykkää melusta.* (I don't like noise.) |

## Cultural Insight

Did you know that Finns are very direct communicators? They generally dislike ambiguity and prefer straightforwardness. When a Finn says they like something, they genuinely mean it. Similarly, if they express a dislike, it's not meant to be rude, but rather an honest statement of their preference. This directness is often appreciated once you understand its cultural context.
